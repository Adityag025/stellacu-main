The 9-agent stack we deployed inside a marketing team in 6 weeks.

Field Report Agentic AI AI & Automation

The company was a B2B SaaS firm running a 14-person marketing team. The CMO had a hiring freeze, a board mandate to deliver 30% more output, and a budget for tooling but not headcount.

We deployed a 9-agent operational stack inside the team in 6 weeks. Six months in, the team has not hired anyone, has shipped 42% more output, and has cut three contractors.

The 9 agents and what each does

1. Brief writer. Drafts the initial creative brief from a one-paragraph kickoff. Saves 90 minutes per brief.

2. Research aggregator. Pulls market, competitor, and audience data from 14 connected sources, summarizes daily. Replaces 60% of manual research hours.

3. Editorial calendar manager. Owns the publishing pipeline, flags slippages, auto-generates status updates.

4. SEO outline generator. Builds research-backed content briefs with target queries, semantic clusters, and competitive gaps. Cuts content brief time from 4 hours to 30 minutes.

5. Ad copy variant generator. Generates 6-12 ad variants per ad set with hypothesis tags. Increased testing velocity 3x.

6. Analytics summarizer. Pulls daily and weekly performance summaries with anomaly callouts. Eliminated Monday morning reporting standup.

7. Email lifecycle agent. Drafts and tests lifecycle email variants based on engagement signals.

8. CRM hygiene agent. Cleans, deduplicates, and enriches CRM data continuously. Improved lead-data accuracy from 67% to 94%.

9. QA agent. Reviews all outbound content against brand guidelines, legal compliance, and tone consistency before publish. Prevented 9 brand-risk incidents in 6 months.

The build sequence

Phase 1 (weeks 1-2): Foundation. We connected the team's existing tools (HubSpot, Google Analytics, Ahrefs, Hotjar, Figma, Asana) to a unified data layer. The foundation layer is what most stacks miss.

Phase 2 (weeks 3-4): The 5 highest-leverage agents. Brief writer, research aggregator, SEO outline generator, analytics summarizer, ad copy variant generator. These five paid back the entire engagement cost by month 2.

Phase 3 (weeks 5-6): The supporting agents. The other four extended the stack from "saves time" to "operates independently."

What worked

One. We treated the team as the design point, not the technology. Every agent was built from a workflow the team already did. We didn't build agents for things we thought were cool. We built agents for things the team complained about doing.

Two. We built one agent fully before building the next. Most agent stacks fail because teams try to build 9 agents in parallel and ship none of them. We shipped one agent per week.

Three. We trained the team to maintain, not just use. Every agent has a documented prompt, a known failure mode, and a human owner on the team.

What didn't work the first time

We initially built the brief writer to "be creative." It produced briefs too vague to act on. We rebuilt it to "be specific" and gave it a strict template. The lesson: agents should be specialists, not generalists.

We also initially gave the QA agent too much veto power. It blocked legitimate creative experiments. We rebuilt it to flag rather than block.

What this engagement taught us

One. Most marketing teams have 6-9 high-leverage agent opportunities already in their workflow. They don't need a new strategy. They need an audit.

Two. The foundation layer is the determinant of success. Teams that build agent stacks without a unified data layer end up with disconnected tools.

Three. Agents are easy. Adoption is hard. Half the engagement was about change management.

If your team has hit a ceiling on output and the next move is to hire, the agent stack is usually the better answer.
