Three OOH campaigns that survived past week 2, and what they shared.

Teardown OOH Media & Growth

Most OOH campaigns die at week 2. Not literally (the units run their full booking), but the cultural attention they generated in week 1 has faded by the end of week 2. The campaign keeps spending money. Audiences have stopped noticing.

Some campaigns don't die at week 2. They build through week 4 and become reference cases for years afterward. We studied three of them. Here's what they shared.

Case 1: A challenger fintech brand, Mumbai

A fintech challenger ran a single-message OOH campaign across 80 sites. The message: "Your bank charges you to send money. We don't." Three sites had handwritten-style additions added in week 2. Five more by week 3. By week 4, the campaign was on every consumer Twitter feed because people were noticing the additions.

The brand had pre-staged the additions as a rolling reveal. It looked like organic spread. It wasn't. It was sequenced.

Case 2: An automotive launch, Bangalore

An automotive brand launched a flagship car with billboards showing only the car's silhouette and the words "First 1000." For 14 days, that was the entire creative.

On day 15, every billboard updated to "947." Day 16, "812." By day 21, the counter was at "312" and consumer attention was peak. The reveal: the first 1000 buyers got pre-launch pricing.

The campaign worked because it created a clock.

Case 3: A premium FMCG brand, Delhi

A premium tea brand ran 24 transit shelter ads featuring 24 different teas, each with a single sensory descriptor: "Tastes like Sunday morning." "Smells like a Monday won." "Looks like the day finally cleared up."

Audiences started photographing them and posting which one they'd seen. Frequency was driven by social, not by media buy.

What the three campaigns shared

Pattern one. The campaign extended past week 2 with something new. The campaign event in week 1 was never the only event.

Pattern two. The creative gave audiences a reason to talk. All three produced screenshot-able moments. The earned media multiple far exceeded the paid spend.

Pattern three. The brand was the answer, not the question. None led with the brand name. The product was the puzzle. By the time audiences knew the brand, they wanted to.

The brief most OOH campaigns get wrong

Most OOH briefs ask: "What's the message?" The three campaigns above answered a different question: "What's the event we're staging in week 1, week 2, week 3, and week 4?"

OOH is a media format. It runs for weeks. Treating it like a print ad gets you a week of attention. Treating it like a TV show gets you a month.
