How we cut a consumer electronics brand's media carbon footprint by 40% without losing reach.

Field Report Eco Media Carbon-Smart

The CMO had a sustainability commitment in the company's annual investor report that her media plan was actively undermining. The brand, a premium consumer electronics company with a strong D2C arm and a national retail footprint, was running roughly $2M/year in digital paid across YouTube, Meta, programmatic display, and connected TV. The reach was holding. The carbon cost of that media was ballooning faster than her ESG reporting was tracking.

She asked a simple question: "How do I cut the carbon cost of my media without cutting the reach?"

This is the field report on what we found, what we changed, and a 40% footprint reduction with no measurable performance loss.

The audit

The first thing we did was measure the existing footprint. Most CMOs don't know their digital media has a measurable carbon cost. It does. Every ad impression triggers data transfer, server compute, ad-tech routing, and end-device load. Each step uses energy.

The benchmark in the industry is roughly 1.2 grams of CO2e per 1000 impressions for well-optimized campaigns. The brand we audited was running at 4.8 grams per 1000 impressions. They were 4x worse than baseline.

We mapped the source of the overage:

  • 30% from heavy creative. Their hero product videos averaged 24MB per asset, with multiple format variants per campaign. The industry standard for performant video creative is 4 to 8MB.
  • 25% from inefficient delivery. They were running 14-second pre-rolls when 6 seconds would convert at the same rate. Premium product storytelling had crept into a format meant for direct response.
  • 20% from ad-tech routing waste. Their programmatic stack was passing impressions through 11 hops on average. Industry efficient is 3 to 5.
  • 15% from over-frequency. Lifetime frequency caps were set at 14 per user, well past the point of diminishing returns for an electronics purchase cycle.
  • 10% from inefficient placements. They were buying inventory on data-center-heavy CTV and video properties when equivalent reach was available on lighter alternatives.

The 5 moves

Move 1: Lean creative build. We compressed all video assets through modern codecs and stripped unnecessary metadata. Average asset weight dropped from 24MB to 7MB. No quality loss visible to viewers, which matters for an electronics brand where product fidelity is part of the story.

Move 2: Format optimization. We shortened pre-rolls from 14 to 6 seconds, kept the 30-second hero spots for brand campaigns, and re-cut the longer assets for retail-media placements only. Conversion held across all formats. Carbon cost dropped 57% on the pre-roll line item alone.

Move 3: Ad-tech path consolidation. We worked with their programmatic partner to reduce average hops from 11 to 4. This was the most political move (it cut some incumbent vendors out of the supply chain) but produced the largest single reduction in carbon cost.

Move 4: Frequency rationalization. We capped lifetime frequency at 8. Performance was statistically unchanged. 35% reduction in wasted impressions.

Move 5: Green inventory selection. We worked with SSP partners to prioritize inventory on properties with renewable-powered infrastructure where available. Where it wasn't, we deprioritized the highest-emission inventory sources from the buy.

The results

  • Total carbon footprint reduction: 41%
  • Reach: unchanged within 2% margin of error
  • Conversion: unchanged within 1% margin of error
  • Media cost: down 8% (frequency caps and path consolidation produced incidental cost savings)
  • Brand recall: unchanged in tracking studies
  • The CMO now has a defensible sustainability metric in every board and investor update

What this engagement taught us

One. Most digital media plans are 3 to 5x more carbon-intensive than they need to be. Not because anyone is doing anything wrong. Because nobody is measuring it. What gets measured gets reduced.

Two. Carbon reduction and cost reduction usually move together. Lean creative, fewer hops, capped frequency, and efficient inventory are all also cheaper. There's no trade-off between sustainability and performance when you're starting from a baseline as wasteful as most brands are.

Three. The hardest part is political, not technical. The 5 moves above are technically straightforward. The hard part is having the conversation with the ad-tech vendor whose hops you're cutting out and the agency that's been buying inefficient inventory by habit. The companies that succeed are the ones whose CMO is willing to have those conversations.

If your sustainability commitments are in the board deck and the investor report but not in the media plan, the gap will get noticed in the next 24 months. Better to close it on your terms now than have to close it under pressure later.
