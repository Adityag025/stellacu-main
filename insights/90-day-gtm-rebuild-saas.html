Inside our 90-day GTM rebuild for a Series-B SaaS company.

Field Report GTM Business Advisory

When we walked into the engagement, the company had raised $32M in Series B nine months earlier, doubled their team, and tripled their marketing spend. Revenue growth had flattened. The CEO's pitch to us was simple: "We're spending more than ever and growing less than ever. We don't know what's broken."

This is the field report on what we found, what we changed, and what it taught us about scaling GTM at a critical inflection.

What was actually broken

The temptation when growth stalls is to blame the channels. The CMO had already burned through that hypothesis. They had switched paid agencies twice in the previous year. Neither helped. Creative had been refreshed three times. CTR was up, conversion was down.

The actual problem wasn't channels. It was that the channels were running ads for an old company. The company had pivoted positioning at Series A from a self-serve product to a sales-led enterprise platform. The website had been updated. The pitch deck had been updated. The press releases had been updated. The ads had not.

The campaigns were still selling to a $99-per-month buyer, but the sales team was qualifying $50,000 contracts. Marketing was generating leads the company no longer wanted. Sales was wasting cycles disqualifying them.

"We're spending more than ever and growing less than ever. We don't know what's broken."

The CEO

This is the most common GTM failure we see in our practice. Companies pivot strategy and forget to pivot the marketing operating system.

The 90-day plan

We don't normally write "90-day plans." Marketing is a long game. But the board had a 90-day patience window. So we scoped a phased intervention.

Days 1 to 14: Market Diagnosis

We re-ran the buyer persona work. New segmentation. New ideal customer profile. New competitive frame. The output was a single-page document showing the company's positioning gap: who they were selling to, who they were talking to in market, and the daylight between the two.

Days 15 to 30: Messaging Architecture

We rebuilt the messaging from scratch. Not a tagline refresh. A messaging stack: category positioning, three product narratives, segment-specific value propositions, proof points, and the standard objections. Crucially, we wrote it as a system the sales team could pull from. Marketing's creative had to compile from the same source files as sales' decks.

Days 31 to 60: Channel Realignment

We didn't change the channel mix. We changed what the channels were saying. New paid social variants, new landing page tracks, new outbound sequences, new sales enablement assets. All compiled from the messaging architecture. We also killed two channels that were still chasing the old buyer. Display retargeting and a partnership program with an SMB-focused integrator both got switched off. Annual savings: $480,000.

Days 61 to 90: Conversion Hardening

We rebuilt the entire post-click experience. New homepage. New solution pages. New nurture tracks. New SQL definition with sales. New lead scoring model that reflected enterprise intent signals: firmographic match, multi-stakeholder engagement, repeat session depth.

What happened

By day 90:

  • Marketing-sourced pipeline volume was down 22 percent
  • Marketing-sourced pipeline value was up 41 percent
  • SQL-to-opportunity conversion was up from 12 percent to 31 percent
  • The sales team stopped asking marketing to "just send more"
  • The CEO stopped asking marketing to defend the budget

The board approved the next 12-month budget at the following quarterly review.

What this engagement taught us

Three things we now apply to every GTM rebuild that follows.

One. When growth stalls after a pivot, the problem is almost always system misalignment, not channel underperformance. Audit the system before you change the channels.

Two. Messaging architecture is plumbing. It only earns its keep when sales, marketing, product, and CS all compile from it. If only marketing uses it, you haven't built architecture. You've written a manifesto.

Three. A 90-day window forces choices. We had to kill two channels to fund the rebuild. Slower engagements drift. Tight ones force discipline.

If your company is in a similar moment, the diagnosis matters more than the action plan. We would rather spend three weeks finding the right gap to close than three months executing on the wrong one. That's what every Growth Audit is for.
